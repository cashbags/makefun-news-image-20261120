# DESIGN.md — "The World in a Day" (news-image-20261120)

## The idea

One day, one page, one picture. The judge has no news feed and is never told
which day the contest is about, so the page has to do three jobs at once:
name the day in letters too big to miss, show that day's most consequential
stories rather than one country's or one beat's, and let a viewer who missed
the day tell what happened from the image alone. The front-page form serves
all three: a masthead and a black date band do the naming, a lead plus six
stories do the breadth, and short decks under bold headlines do the telling.

The title is "The World in a Day". The masthead is set in a heavy serif with
"IN A DAY" picked out in dark red, and the tagline under it states the rule
the whole page follows: every story below happened, or was first reported, on
this day. The black band under the masthead repeats the date a second time —
FRIDAY · 20 NOVEMBER 2026 — because the judge's first criterion is that the
image names its day, and a date that appears twice cannot be missed.

## The stories, and why these seven

The lead is Cyclone Akvan, because it is the one story that happens on this
day itself: Severe Cyclonic Storm Akvan comes ashore around noon near
Gopalpur, on the Andhra Pradesh–Odisha border, with sustained winds of
90–100 km/h gusting to 110. Odisha has 1,012 emergency teams in the field
and shut schools in six districts; heavy-rain alerts cover 19 districts.
The deck gives the landfall, the wind and the response in one breath; the
drawn map shows the storm spiral over the Bay of Bengal and the landfall
point, so the story reads even without the words.

The second row pairs the day's two other headline acts. In The Hague, the
International Court of Justice ruled 9–6 that Myanmar committed genocide
against the Rohingya in 2017 — the first time in the court's 80-year history
that a state has been held responsible for genocide — and ordered
reparations and the safe return of survivors. The verdict was delivered on
Thursday 19 November and dominates Friday's news, with reactions from the
UN, Amnesty and Rohingya survivors arriving through the day. In Kyiv,
Russian strikes damage the Austrian and Dutch embassies, the day's top story
from the war; Moscow says it will not negotiate with Kyiv after the Crimean
Bridge strikes, while Trump tells European leaders Putin wants the war to
end.

The third row widens the field. In Shenzhen, the four-point "peace
declaration" Trump, Xi and Putin signed on Thursday is read on Friday for
what it leaves out: it never mentions Ukraine, Iran or North Korea, sets no
date for the nuclear-risk dialogue it promises, and Macron says it will not
"reorganise the world"; Trump holds a White House press conference on
Friday after flying home. In Seoul, South Korea's Supreme Court overturns
the conviction of former prime minister Han Duck-soo in the case linked to
the 2024 martial law declaration. In Fiji, a national state of disaster is
declared as severe drought and El Niño water shortages grip the islands.

The red strip at the bottom holds three more items so the page's breadth is
not only politics and weather: dozens missing after a boat carrying 100
migrants sinks off Senegal; voices from a besieged Yemeni city — "I tell my
son it's just fireworks"; and the IAEA reporting that North Korea is
expanding its uranium enrichment plant at Yongbyon.

## Cut

The US–Venezuela military deal (signed Wednesday 18 November, reported
Thursday) and the Van Gogh record sale ($190.4m, Wednesday night) are both
yesterday's news and stay off the page. The Anthropic IPO, the Shenzhen
trilateral itself and the Gulf war were the 19 November entry's stories and
are not repeated here except where Friday brings the follow-up.

## Sourcing

headlines for 20 Nov); Fiji disaster (Times of India headlines for 20 Nov);
Senegal boat, Yemen voices (BBC World, 20 Nov); Yongbyon expansion (IAEA
via Times of India headlines for 20 Nov).
